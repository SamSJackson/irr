## Geotagging the Path of Hurricane Ian 
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijdrr.2023.103865)
This paper discusses using a real-time stream of tweets to understand impact of the hurricane and its path. 

Specifically, they would utilize a stream of tweets, keeping tweets that contain required words, such as "Hurricane" or "Ian" and similar (a little more advanced than that). Then, the geolocation of the tweet, found in the tweet's metadata, is used to map the path of the hurricane. 

This can be extended further and the content of tweets (sentiment analysis) can be used to assist in understanding impact of hurricane.
## Understanding Public Response to Bushfire Disaster
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2023.121274)
This one is more Disaster Relief as opposed to Disaster Response, as found in the previous two papers.

This paper is considered Disaster Relief because the focus is about using Network Analysis alongside Sentiment Analysis to understand the public's opinion of the government's handling of the Australian Bushfires. 

The network analysis techniques revolve around temporal networks and location-based networks. The paper identifies when tweets took place alongside where they took place and incorporate this information to their results.
